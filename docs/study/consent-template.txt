Purpose
We are studying how clinicians choose their next interface action and how much effort four experimental EHR designs require. Our computer models have not been validated against people. This is a software usability pilot, not an assessment of your clinical competence or a clinical treatment study.

What you will do
You will practice four designs, then complete four synthetic emergency-department cases, one per design, and answer brief workload and usability questions after each measured case. Allow about 60–90 minutes including breaks. The facilitator will explain the controls and task goals. You may stop a task, skip any question by ending participation, take a break between blocks, or withdraw at any time.

All patients and clinical records in this activity are synthetic. Do not enter any real patient information, names, contact details, passwords, or other confidential information. Do not use this software to make decisions for real patients.

What we record
With your permission, the local study software records a pseudonymous participant ID, allocation slot, interface actions and synthetic values entered, counts of mouse clicks and key presses, aggregate mouse movement and scrolling, elapsed time and hidden-tab time, blocked actions, task completion, and questionnaire answers. It stores browser viewport dimensions. It does not record audio, video, screen video, individual key values, or your real identity. Synthetic clinical entry values remain in the interaction trace. The facilitator may keep a separate recruitment/contact list outside the study data, under institutional access rules.

Risks and benefits
You may experience fatigue, frustration, or concern about being observed. You may pause between tasks or stop without giving a reason. There is a small privacy risk if local study files are accessed without permission. This is not an employee performance evaluation; supervisors will not receive individual results. There is no expected direct benefit; the results may inform future interface and simulation research.
