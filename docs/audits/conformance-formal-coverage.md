## Effect of screen-local navigation

The earlier audit attributed 196 failures to layouts with a tiny shortcut on `chartSummary` and a usable detour on `directory`. Both screens called their link `toList`. Lean scopes IDs by screen. The driver now excludes only the failed screen/control pair and traverses `toDirectory` followed by `toList` for all five v2 personas. Those historical failures can no longer serve as counterexamples. [Driver and regression test](../../conformance/driver.test.mjs)

Eight rows move to checker coverage with this fix. For an NP in a pre-op encounter the required signing site is reachable and the encounter-role refusal precedes entry validation. Seven injected-default probes have a required, revealable field for every applicable release persona. The checker supplies the route, and the native interpreter suppresses the default and accepts the valid human sample. Those fields are wristband scan, medication scan, high-alert cosign, consent verification, pain reassessment, naloxone offer and naloxone education. The macro probes additionally have to reveal the specific injected control beside the first matching field. A hidden duplicate could prevent that before the entry trace check. [Reachable required fields](../../lean/Ehr/Check/Rules.lean) · [Guard order](../../lean/Ehr/Core/Guard.lean) · [Human-only prefills](../../lean/Ehr/Proofs/T3.lean)

Two more rows move with the new checks. A surfaced prior authorization field now has a human-entry route even without an existing service composer. The patient-switch scenario uses the medication workflow's identity field and pinned allergy list together with native clearing, binding and FHIR filtering. It also attempts the previous patient's identity and a premature medication commit on the new chart. [Workflow soundness](../../lean/Ehr/Check/WorkflowSound.lean) · [Selection scenario](../../conformance/selection.mjs)

## Remaining work in clinical risk order

1. **Patient identity and allergy review.** The checker supplies the current chart's identity route and shows allergies at medication commit. The runtime checks the identity value and restores only the selected patient's draft. The ordinary signing witness now completes those prerequisites. Wrong-patient attempts and cross-patient completion still need their own composed traces.
2. **Dose correction and later medication lines.** Acceptance supplies editable drug and dose fields before and after line advancement. Native advancement and signing check each applicable line, and correction preserves other lines' doses. The controlled prescribing, antibiotic and weight fixtures now have successful two-line or corrected-weight witnesses. The other unit-specific dose tests still need traces for their own wrong-unit attempt and native-unit recovery.
3. **Critical-result follow-through.** Acceptance supplies a same-workflow result action. The displayed unresolved ticket has a clinical action field that the responsible clinician can use on the bound chart. The full test also signs the inbox, exercises timed delivery, suppression and resolution. Several independently proved transitions must compose on that trace.
4. **Service ordering.** The checker covers both directions of opt-in, the declared service fields and status, and a drug-free route. Whole-scenario assertions about request handling and alerts remain above.
5. **Completion and actionable refusals.** Release entry fixtures now cover the named refusal, confirmed entries and signature. Review suppression, receipt variants, other alert-trigger fixtures and completion from arbitrary feasible states remain open.
6. **Landmarks.** The checker pins a patient banner. The suite additionally asks for exactly one shown banner on each visited screen.

The existing feasibility obstruction demonstrates why structural completability does not establish positive completion for an arbitrary accepted layout.
