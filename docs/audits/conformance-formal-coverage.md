The unit of coverage is **every assertion of an applicable scenario**, including setup through shown controls, named refusal reasons and any successful recovery or commit. The comparison fixes the suite’s release, role-specific canonical, persona, clinical facts and native interpreter. A test marked inapplicable contributes no evidence. A proof that all successful commits satisfy a guard establishes safety even when a clinician cannot reach the commit. A witness of a successful human path establishes reachability even when the JS driver misses it. Both distinctions affect the counts. ## Evidence from accepted layouts

A single navigation pattern produced failures in **192 distinct scenarios on v2**. Applying it to v3 P1 and P3 added three antibiotic scenarios. Applying it to frozen core-10day P1 v0 added the older removable high-alert notice, giving **196 distinct applicable failures**. Each input passed native `check` with its original canonical and previous program. These are counterexamples to universal conformance. The route failure itself establishes no unsafe signature or patient harm.

To reproduce, start with the indicated persona, preserve its selection flag and existing clinical screens, and change the buttons on `chartSummary`. Keep its banner and nonbutton components. Give it a tiny goto button named `toList` targeting the program’s start screen, followed by a normal goto named `toDirectory` targeting a new `directory` screen. Give the new screen workflow `chart`, a banner and a normal goto named `toList` to the start screen. Use always-visible components at tab zero and the target titles as button labels. The native human route is `toDirectory`, then `toList`. After the tiny shortcut cannot be used, the driver excludes its ID on *both* screens. Lean component IDs are unique within a screen. The earliest missing control reported by a scenario therefore often belongs to its setup. Scope unavailable navigation controls by screen and validate traversal against actual shown native transitions before using failures to estimate clinical risk. ## Proposed rules in clinical risk order

1. **Patient identity and cross-chart continuity.** Require a usable identity or barcode entry and patient-scoped allergy display before and after chart switches. Connect source binding, draft isolation and fresh captures to the actual displayed data. A blocked check or an unavailable review can delay bedside decisions.
2. **Medication dose, subsequent lines and controlled prescribing.** Require reachable add-line and correction actions for medication workflows. Witness each line’s native unit, weight source, antibiotic indication, refills and applicable opioid safeguards through successful signing. Render the controlled line’s details as structured values at the point of signature.
3. **Critical results and medication alerts.** Witness the responsible clinician’s receipt, acknowledgement, documented action and resolution with urgent display until resolution. For interruptive alerts, prove progress after the correct acknowledgement and eligibility to interrupt on clinically changed data.
4. **Service ordering and access to authorization.** Define which controls opt a workflow into service ordering. Require both order discriminators and a drug-free service-only path through human request entry, with repeat requests preserving the confirmed value. Missing authorization can delay an indicated study or procedure.
5. **Completion, review and actionable refusals.** For each supported task, check constructive human progress through its feasible entries, applicable reviews, modals and decision alternatives. For each negative case, witness the exact refusal after its legitimate prefix. Exercise human actions as well as blocked automation.
6. **Screen landmarks and test traversal.** Require one shown patient banner per reachable screen. Scope the driver’s visited controls to their screen and verify that it can follow native witness paths. This improves confidence in measurement across every row affected by navigation.

The risk ranking concerns what a missing guarantee could prevent clinicians from doing. Evidence of a failed driver path alone does not establish an incorrect medication dose, an unauthorized signature or a cross-patient disclosure. The current completability rule checks structural paths and prerequisite coverage. A separately verified scenario witness has stronger execution hypotheses. The existing feasibility obstruction explains why ordinary acceptance cannot simply be cited for positive completion.
